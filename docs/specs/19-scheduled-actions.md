# 19 · Scheduled actions

Added after 1.0.23. Until then an action (05) ran only when its button was pressed, and views (04) ran on their own every so many minutes with nothing on the board to show it. Now an action can also run on a **schedule**, such as every weekday at 9, and a scheduled action gets a **tile** on its project's board: a small panel with a clock whose ring fills toward the next run, what it last said, and its runs coming out beside it along a dotted line. Views' schedules get the same clock in their caption, and a picker to change them without asking Claude.

## Schedules

- **SCHED-001** A project's own action may have a `schedule`, written after `createdAt` and left out when it has none: `{ words?, days?, at?, every?, paused? }`.
  - `words` is what the user typed (up to 120 UTF-16 units), kept so the When field shows it again.
  - `days` are the days it runs on, 0 for Sunday to 6 for Saturday, in order and once each; left out (or all seven) means every day.
  - `at` is the time of day it runs, `HH:MM` on a 24-hour clock, local time. `every` is minutes between runs instead, counted from midnight on each day it runs on. A schedule has one or the other; `at` wins when both are sent.
  - `paused` is only `true`.
  - What the window sends is checked: days outside 0–6 dropped, an `at` that isn't a time dropped, `every` clamped (SCHED-003). A schedule with neither `at` nor `every` is read from its `words`; one that still says no time is left out.
- **SCHED-002** Words are read without Claude, case aside: days by name or short name (`monday`, `mon`, `mondays`), ranges (`mon-fri`, `monday to friday`, wrapping past Sunday), `weekdays` / `workdays` / `business days` (Monday to Friday), `weekends`, `daily` / `every day`, and `except` days. Times: `9`, `9:30`, `9.30`, `0930`, `5pm`, `12am`, `7 p.m.`, `noon`, `midnight`, `morning` (9:00), `afternoon` (14:00), `evening` (18:00), `night` (21:00). Hours without am or pm read as a 24-hour clock. Every so often: `hourly`, `every hour`, `every 2 hours`, `every 30 minutes`, `every 15m`, `every half hour`, `every other hour`.
- **SCHED-003** `every` is whole minutes, at least 10 (each run is a Claude session) and at most 720; rarer than twice a day is a time of day.
- **SCHED-004** Days with no time run at 9:00. Words that say nothing about when (`review the PRs`) are no schedule.
- **SCHED-005** A schedule with an `every` has no `at`, and the other way round.
- **SCHED-006** A schedule is said back as `Weekdays at 9:00`, `Every day at 17:30`, `Weekends at 10:00`, `Mondays at 9:00`, `Mon, Wed and Fri at 9:00`, `Every 2 hours`, `Every hour on weekdays`, `Every 90 minutes on Fridays`. Times are `H:MM`. What it says reads back as the same schedule.
- **SCHED-007** A global action has no schedule: saving one global drops it. A scheduled action belongs to one project's board.
- **SCHED-008** The drafter's answer has a `when` too (`"when":{"type":"string"}`, required): the words for when the action should also run on its own if the user's description says so (`every weekday at 9`), else empty, and never in the name or the prompt. The system prompt says so in one more bullet after `prompt`. A `when` that reads as a schedule turns the dialog's schedule on, unless the action is global.

## When it runs

- **SCHED-010** A schedule runs at each of its times on each day it runs on, in local time. A time daylight saving skips (2:30 on the night the clocks go forward) runs an hour later that day; one it repeats runs at the first.
- **SCHED-020** Every 30 seconds while the app is open, the first time 30 seconds after launch (when the session host's runs are known), the core looks at every scheduled action of every local project that isn't archived. Each tile remembers how far its schedule is handled (`handled`, ms): when the tile was made, the schedule last changed or was resumed, or the last time it was due. An action is due when the latest of its times at or before now is after that. Then:
  - Its tile is handled up to that time first, so a slow start isn't started twice.
  - If a run of it is still on the board and live, that time is skipped.
  - Otherwise a run starts, as a press of its button does: a session on its prompt in auto mode, with its name and the run system prompt.
  - A paused schedule is never due. A server board's schedules run on the server (spec 18), which has its own ticker.
- **SCHED-021** Only the latest time missed runs, once: an app closed from Tuesday to Thursday runs Thursday's 9:00 when it opens, not three. A run that starts more than 5 minutes after its time is **late**, and its tile says so.
- **SCHED-022** A run that can't start (no `claude`, the host down) puts `Couldn’t start: <why>` on its tile as its result. It isn't tried again until the next time.
- **SCHED-023** A tile keeps its last run: `{ sessionId, at, due?, late?, doneAt?, message?, seen? }`. A scheduled run has the time it was `due` for; a press of the button has none, and shows on the tile too. When the run's turn ends (05's finish), the tile keeps when (`doneAt`) and Claude's last words (`message`), and the result is unread (`seen` left out). The run leaves the board as any run does, and the toast shows as before.

## Tiles

- **SCHED-024** A project's board has a tile for each of its scheduled actions and no others (`tiles` in `state.json`, keyed by the action's id, a lenient record map as shapes are, left out while empty). Saving an action, and opening the board, bring them in line: one with a new schedule gets a tile, one whose schedule is taken off (or that is deleted, or turned global) loses it. A schedule that was changed or resumed is handled from now (SCHED-020), so it doesn't run for a time that passed while it was different or paused. The board is told it changed.
- **SCHED-025** A new tile goes beside the rightmost card, else right of the dock, in the nearest free spot (`findSpotBeside`, `findFreeSpot`). Tiles are obstacles for notes, views and drawings placed from then on (NOTE-005).
- **SCHED-026** A run of a scheduled action, by its schedule or its button, comes out beside its tile, clear of the board's things, and, unlike other runs (ACT-032), it's drawn on the board as a card while it runs. Taking it over makes it an ordinary card (ACT-043) that stays where it is.
- **SCHED-030** A tile is 288×136 board units, a rounded panel (radius 12) on `bg-elevated` with the small card shadow, an accent ring while selected or dragged. Top: a 22-unit clock whose ring fills toward the next run, the action's name (14, semibold), its schedule said back (12, secondary) and a state line (12, tertiary). Under a hairline: its last run.
- **SCHED-031** The state line: `Running now` (with the run's status dot) while a run of it is on the board, `Paused`, or `Next in 2h 14m` within six hours, else `Next at 17:00` later today, `Next tomorrow at 9:00`, `Next Tue at 9:00`.
- **SCHED-032** The ring fills from the last time it was due to the next, in the accent; full while it runs; a paused tile's clock has no ring. The board is drawn again every 20 seconds while it has tiles.
- **SCHED-033** A tile is dragged like a note and saved where it lands (`board:move-tile`, rounded). Dropped on the archive or the note pad, it goes back. It isn't put in frames or groups, and links don't hold it.
- **SCHED-034** The last run: Claude's last words (one line, cut with `…`; `Finished` when there are none), and under them when it ran: `9:00`, `yesterday 9:00` or `Tue 9:00`, with ` · ran late, Nodus was closed` or ` · run by hand`. An unread result has an accent dot before it and primary text; a read one, secondary text and no dot. With no runs yet it says `No runs yet.`; while the first runs, `Its run is beside it.`
- **SCHED-035** A tile's menu: `Run now` (or `Show run` while one is on the board), `Open last run` when there's one, then `Edit action…` (the action dialog, as the rail's `Edit…`), `Pause schedule` / `Resume schedule`, then `Remove schedule` (red), which takes the schedule off and the tile with it; the action stays in the rail.
- **SCHED-036** Clicking the last run, a double-click on the tile, or `Open last run` opens it: the run's card while it's on the board, else its transcript in the read-only preview (12). Its result is then read (`board:see-tile`).
- **SCHED-037** A dotted line (as BOARD-184's) runs from a tile to its run while the run is on the board. When the run comes out, a light runs along it from the tile and the line draws in behind it; when the run's turn ends, a light runs back (MOTION-044's light).

## The action dialog

- **SCHED-040** The review step has a **When** row between the prompt and `Use in every project`: the label and a two-way switch, `When I press it` | `On a schedule`. Turning the schedule on turns the global switch off, and the other way round (SCHED-007). Turned on with no schedule yet, it starts as `every weekday at 9`.
- **SCHED-041** On a schedule, a field holds the schedule in words (placeholder `every weekday at 9`). As the user types, words that say when replace the schedule; words that don't leave it as it was, and the line under the row says `Not sure when that is. Try “every weekday at 9” or “every 2 hours”.`
- **SCHED-042** Under the field, seven day chips, Monday first (`M T W T F S S`; on: accent outline and letter), then `at` and a time field. A chip turns its day on or off (the last day stays on); a time typed in (`9`, `9:30`, `17:05`) makes it run once a day at that time. Either way the words field then says the schedule back (SCHED-006), so the two agree. The time field shows the schedule's time once the user leaves it.
- **SCHED-043** Saved, the action has the schedule as Nodus read it, with the words. Off, it has none.
- **SCHED-044** The line under the row says the schedule back and when it next runs: `Weekdays at 9:00 · next tomorrow at 9:00`.

## Views

- **SCHED-060** Words for a view's schedule, which only runs every so often, are read as minutes: `every 15 minutes`, `15m`, a bare number, `hourly`, `every 2 hours`, `every half hour`, `daily`, `every day`, `every 3 days`, `every other day`, `weekly`. Seconds are no schedule. Views keep their own limits (VIEW-011).
- **SCHED-061** `views:schedules` lists a view's schedules for its caption: one for each command with an `every`, in the commands' order (key `command:<name>`, labelled with its name), then one for its refresh (key `refresh`, labelled `Claude’s refresh`). Each is `{ key, label, every, paused, allowed, nextAt }`. `allowed` says the user allowed the command's command line as it is now; a refresh always is. A schedule runs by itself when it's allowed and not paused. On a server board, the server answers.
- **SCHED-062** `nextAt` is `every` minutes after the last run: for a command, its last result's `at`, or now when it hasn't run; for a refresh, its last refresh (`refreshedAt`), or when the view was made.
- **SCHED-063** `views:set-every` `{ projectId, viewId, key, every }` changes one schedule in place: a command's `every` (rounded, at least 1 minute) or the refresh's (at least 10), at most a week (VIEW-011). Nothing else about the command changes. Its approval stands, since its command line is the same, and fields this version doesn't know are kept. The board is told it changed. Errors: `How often must be a number of minutes`, `That command is gone`, `This view doesn’t refresh`, `This view has no schedule called “<key>”`.
- **SCHED-064** `every: null` turns a schedule off: the command or refresh gets `paused: true` (only `true` is written) and keeps its interval and prompt. The 30-second ticker (VIEW-085) skips it, but the page can still run the command and `Refresh` still asks the brain. Picking an interval turns it on again; if its last run is longer ago than the interval, it's due at the next tick. Commands and refreshes that Claude sets (`create_view`, `update_view`) start on. `get_view` says `"paused": true` for a command that's off. The approvals strip doesn't say how often an off command would run.
- **SCHED-065** A view with a schedule has a clock after its title in the caption. The clock is 14 units, and its ring fills in the accent from the soonest running schedule's last run to its next. Next to it, in 11-unit tertiary tabular figures, is when that run is: `in 12m`, `in 1h 5m`, `in 40s`, or `due` once its time has passed and the ticker hasn't started it. When nothing runs (off, waiting to be allowed, or turned down), the clock has no ring and says `Off`. There's no other colour. Its tooltip says `Change how often it updates`. Under 18 px a ringed clock has only its hands.
- **SCHED-066** Clicking the clock selects the view, as its buttons do, and opens the picker under it as a menu. The page shows its snapshot while the picker is open. Clicking the clock again, Esc, or a press anywhere else closes it, and the board gets the keyboard back if the picker's field had it.
- **SCHED-067** The picker has a row for each schedule. Each row shows its label (12, medium) with how often it runs now on the right (`every 15 minutes`, `Off`, or `Not allowed to run`), over a row of chips. A command's chips are `1m 5m 15m 30m 1h 6h 1d Off`; a refresh's are `10m 15m 30m 1h 6h 1d Off`. The chip for its current interval, or `Off` while it's off, has the accent outline and text. A chip sets the interval at once and the picker stays open. A failure shows on the view's error line. An interval with no chip (`every 2 hours`) shows only in the words on the right.
- **SCHED-068** A view with one schedule has a field above its row, focused when the picker opens (placeholder `Type how often: every 2 hours`). Enter reads the words as SCHED-060 does; `off`, `never`, `pause` and `stop` turn it off. It then sets the interval and empties the field. Words it can't read leave the schedule as it is, and the last line says `Try “every 2 hours”, “15m” or “daily”` until the field changes.
- **SCHED-069** The picker's last line, under a hairline, is `Next in 12m` (the soonest running schedule), `Due now`, or `Nothing runs on its own`.
- **SCHED-070** The caption reads the schedules again when the view's commands or refresh change, when a command's result reaches the board, after a change from the picker, every 5 seconds while a run is due, and at least once a minute. While something runs, the caption is drawn again every second when the soonest schedule runs every 5 minutes or less or within 90 seconds, otherwise every 5 seconds. It isn't redrawn while everything is off, or while the clock is frozen (parity shots, D11).
